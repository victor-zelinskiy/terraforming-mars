## Промт (один блок, скопировать целиком)

```
You are illustrating the CORPORATION card "Proteus" for a fan expansion of the board game Terraforming Mars. Attached: the card scan — copy the corporate logo from its top-left corner. Generate ONE finished illustration right away, without asking questions.

WHO THEY ARE. Proteus was founded by a co-founder of the crowdfunding giant Vitor who left after a dispute over strategy: the other leaders wanted to focus on Mars, he wanted to focus on the ICE MOONS of the outer solar system. Proteus mines water ice on those moons and ships it inward — the water that becomes Martian oceans, the supplies that keep the colonies alive — and it still funds every expedition the Vitor way: with crowdfunding campaigns backed by thousands of ordinary people, whose names it carries into space. Its people are drillers, logistics crews and engineers working far from the sun, on contracts their backers paid for.

THE PICTURE. A wide view of Proteus' main ice-harvesting station on the surface of an ice moon of a giant planet, during a working shift. Underfoot and to the horizon, a plain of brilliant white water ice, fractured into ridges and pressure seams, scuffed grey where vehicles have driven. The station stands on the ice: a row of squat pressurised habitat and workshop modules on jacked legs, linked by covered walkways; a tall ice-coring derrick driving a core into the glacier; a conveyor carrying cut ice blocks into a crusher-and-tank module; a stubby cargo lander on a landing pad being loaded with water tanks for the long haul to Mars, its cargo doors open; a half-assembled colony habitat module on a trailer, waiting for its launch. Real people in bulky EVA suits with lit visors: a drill crew checking the core, a loader driver, two technicians fastening a tank in the lander, a crew member walking the covered walkway with a case — and in the lit window of the habitat, a few people in shirtsleeves around a table. The sky is black, with hard stars; low over the horizon a small, intensely bright but distant sun throws long cold shadows across the ice; above the station hangs the huge pale-blue banded disc of the giant planet, filling a quarter of the sky, lit from the same side. Only one detail tells the crowdfunding story, to be found on the second look: the outer wall of the main habitat, beside the airlock, is covered edge to edge with thousands of small riveted name plates in neat rows — the backers' wall — and a suited worker is fixing a new row of plates to it.

THE LOGO — the centre of the image. Painted on the flank of the main habitat module, in the exact centre of the frame, about one quarter of the image width: the Proteus logo copied faithfully from the scan — the single word "proteus" in soft, rounded lowercase letters, filled in a pale mint green and bounded by a thick dark-navy outline, the whole word enclosed by one dark-navy keyline; the tall letter t carries its crossbar out over its neighbours so the middle letters o-t-e read as one linked group (letters spelled exactly p-r-o-t-e-u-s, no capitals, no extra words). Built as flat durable paint on the module's panels, lit only by the sun and the station's work floodlights — no glow, no backlight, no neon. The wordmark may repeat very small on the lander's tail and on a suit's shoulder patch. No other text, no other logo.

STYLE — match the original Terraforming Mars cards, which are photographs of real places: a photograph or sober engineering visualisation of a real industrial outpost, NOT cinematic concept art; think of a documentary photograph of a real polar drilling station or an Antarctic base at work; standard lens 35–50 mm from standing eye height on the ice, slightly off-axis so the derrick, the conveyor and the walkways run diagonally while the logo stays centred; light only from plausible sources — the hard distant sun, the station's work floodlights on their masts, the habitat windows, a faint blue fill from the planet; NO volumetric god-rays, glowing haze, lens flares, mirror-polished ice or bloom; a cold, restrained palette of white ice, grey-blue shadow, the dull metal and safety-orange trim of the hardware, the black sky, the pale blue of the planet, with the mint green and navy of the logo repeated only in a few small places (a hose, a crate, a visor rim); no teal-and-orange grade, no cyan sci-fi tint; hardware real and USED (frost on the hull seams, cable runs, scuffed paint, tyre tracks, stacked crates, labels without readable text); modest human scale — a work site, not a palace; slight grain, no gloss, no AI sheen. Be bold in the idea and sober in the photograph: the drama is in the thousands of backers' names riveted to a wall at the edge of the solar system, never in effects.

NEVER: a glowing crystal cavern, an underwater scene, aurora, lens flares; a giant sculpted logo; sleek white sci-fi architecture or hovering vehicles; readable text or numbers anywhere; any text other than the Proteus logo; game icons, tokens, cards, cubes or UI; a card frame or border; heroic posing, glamour portraits; a symmetrical vanishing-point avenue; Mars, Earth or the Moon in the sky (this is an ice moon of a giant planet); collage, split screen or inset.

OUTPUT: one landscape image, 1536×1024 (3:2), full bleed. Under the image write one short sentence naming the idea.
```

## Служебное

- **Проверить глазами:** «proteus» строчными, мятная заливка + толстая тёмно-синяя обводка, одно слово без ошибок
  (генераторы пишут PROTEUS капителью или добавляют подпись); логотип один крупный по центру на стене модуля; небо
  чёрное, планета-гигант голубая и ОДНА (не Марс, не Земля); стена спонсоров читается как ряды мелких табличек без
  текста; лёд белый, без «светящегося грота»; нет «AI-открытки» (лучи, зеркальный лёд, осевая симметрия) — иначе
  перегенерить.

  previous frame's light.»
